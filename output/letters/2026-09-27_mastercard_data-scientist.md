## Cover letter

Mastercard's Cyber Analytics team combines cybersecurity and transaction data to find fraud and compromised cards across the network. Payment fraud detection is the focus of my strongest project.

My fraud detection system models customer transaction histories with a GRU, a temporal convolutional network and a causally masked Transformer, benchmarked against a LightGBM baseline on velocity and deviation features. I broke results down by card testing, account takeover and merchant compromise, and measured precision at fixed review capacity. Under an explicit cost model, the sequence models cut expected cost per transaction by 27%, and the served model runs at 5.1 ms p99.

Turning patterns into rules is also familiar. My web3-risk-mcp tool uses explainable rules where every finding has a stable ID and a reason, and it traces funds to mixers, exploiters and phishing addresses. That is close to translating insights into technical rules, as your post asks.

I work in Python and SQL and explain results to non-technical audiences as a machine learning instructor. I hold a B.Sc. in Statistics, can start immediately and am willing to relocate to New York. I would be glad to talk.
