## Cover letter

Wayve's Data Quality and Provenance team builds statistically rigorous ways to decide when annotation, data and evaluation signals can be trusted, and when they cannot. That question runs through my statistics training and my projects.

In my compliance assistant, I built a 50-question labelled golden set and scored retrieval and generation separately. When the offline judge gave a 0.40 refusal-correctness result, I traced it to a limit in the judge itself rather than reporting it as system behaviour, which is the same distinction as evaluation noise against a real quality problem.

In my fraud work, I showed that focal loss degraded calibration to an ECE of 0.0063 while weighted sampling cost 0.042 PR-AUC, then corrected calibration to 0.0005, reporting results across 3 seeds. My web3-risk-mcp project uses 34 hand-checked addresses with a record and replay harness, and my forecasting platform has automated tests that fail the build if any feature leaks past the forecast origin.

I have not worked with multi-rater models such as Dawid-Skene, though my statistics background covers estimation and inference. I can start immediately and am willing to relocate to Germany. I would be glad to talk.

Oluwatobi Melvyn Mayungbo
mlvyn.t@gmail.com

## Why this company

Wayve's driving models are only as good as the data and labels behind them, so knowing which signals to trust is a safety question. Building metrics that hold up to that standard is work I would value.
